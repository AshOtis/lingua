# Lingua

A companion for reading **Lingua Latina per se illustrata** (Familia Romana) in Obsidian.

- **Catch words as you read.** “Add a word” (give the command a hotkey) takes the word as the book gives it (*ancilla*, *puer, puerī*, *pater, patris m.*, *vocat*), its meaning (in Latin if you can), and the sentence you met it in. It goes into today’s daily note under `## Latin`, and into your word list.
- **Review on a spaced schedule.** Words come back when they’re due: Again, Hard, Good or Easy (keys 1–4, space to show).
- **Drill the endings your chapter has taught.** Set the capitulum you’re on and the Forms drill asks only the cases and verb forms the book has introduced by then (nominative in I, genitive in II, accusative and verbs in III…).
- **Endings in your sentences.** Lingua takes the endings off the sentences you saved, for you to put back, a bit like a Pensum A.
- **A Pensa note for each capitulum**, with Pensum A, B and C headings, for your answers to the book’s Pensa.
- **Feed.** Just the Latin sections of your daily notes, newest first. It reads Obsidian’s own index and only the days on screen, so it stays light.
- Long vowels: **Alt + a, e, i, o, u** (or the ā ē ī ō ū buttons). Macrons can count in drills, or not (setting).
- Optional (advanced): **Line of the day**, a short well-known Latin line with a translation to reveal.

Everything is plain Markdown in your vault: your daily notes, `Lingua/Latin words.md` and `Lingua/Pensa/`. Lingua contains no text from the book; you read the book alongside it.

Nothing is sent over the network.

Works well with Lantern Focus, Daily Board and Writers Board.
